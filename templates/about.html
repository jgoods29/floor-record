How we count

{{ site.site_name }} shows what members of the U.S. House did, with a link to the official record for every number. It does not grade anyone or tell you what to think.

Where the data comes from

Votes

Every roll call comes from the Office of the Clerk of the U.S. House. We download each official record and count from it directly. If our count ever differs from the Clerk's published total, the page says so.

Members

Names, districts and terms come from the public-domain congress-legislators project, maintained by volunteers and news organisations.

Bills

Titles, sponsors, policy areas and official summaries come from the Library of Congress through Congress.gov.

What the numbers mean

Party position. On each vote, whichever of Yea or Nay most of a party's voting members chose. If a party split evenly, it had no position on that vote.

Party-split vote. A vote where most Republicans and most Democrats took opposite sides.

With party / against party. How often a member's Yea or Nay matched their own party's position. The headline figures and the leaderboard count only party-split votes, because near-unanimous votes make everyone look the same.

Broke with party. The member voted Yea or Nay and it differed from their party's position on that vote.

Attendance. The share of roll calls where a member voted Yea, Nay or Present. Members miss votes for many reasons, including illness and family emergencies; the record does not say why.

Left out. Quorum calls and the election of the Speaker are listed but not counted in any statistic. Members with fewer than {{ min_votes }} roll calls are not ranked.

Plain-English explanations

What a Yea or Nay meant. These lines come from fixed rules about House procedure. For example, a Yea on a motion to refer always means "send it to committee." They never guess at what a bill contains.

AI-written summaries. Where a page says "AI-written," the summary of what a bill does was drafted by an AI model using only the bill's official title and the Congressional Research Service summary. It is labelled every time, shown next to the official text, and can contain mistakes.
