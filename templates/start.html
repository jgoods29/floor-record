Congress, in two minutes.

You do not need to know anything about politics to use this site. Here is everything you need, in plain English.

1. What is Congress?

Congress is the group of people elected to write the country's laws. It has two halves, and a new law needs a yes from both.

The House435voting members. Each one speaks for one area of a state, called a district. Everyone is up for election every two years.This site covers the House
The Senate100members, called senators. Every state gets two, no matter its size. Each serves six years.Coming to this site later

2. Who is "my representative"?

Wherever you live in the United States, exactly one person in the House represents you. You do not need to have voted for them. Their job is to vote on your behalf, and this site shows you how they have been doing it.

Or browse everyone by state

3. How does an idea become a law?

  1. Introduced
  2. Committee
  3. House
  4. Senate
  5. President
  6. Law

You will see this tracker on every bill. Dark steps are done, purple is where the bill is now, and gray is still ahead.

  1. 1Someone writes a bill. A bill is a proposed law. Any member can write one.
  2. 2A committee looks at it. A small group of members studies it. Most bills stop here and never get a vote.
  3. 3The House votes. Every member says yes or no, on the record. That record is what this site shows.
  4. 4The Senate votes. The other half of Congress has to agree to the same bill.
  5. 5The President signs it. Now it is a law. The President can also say no, which is called a veto.

4. How to read a vote

YeaYes.
NayNo.
Present"I am here, but I am not picking a side."
Did not voteThey were not there, or chose not to vote.

Most votes pass when more than half of the members voting say yea. Each party usually votes together, so the votes where a member goes their own way can tell you a lot.

One thing that trips everyone up: a lot of votes are not about a bill itself. They are about steps along the way, like whether to keep debating or to set a bill aside. On those, a "yea" can mean the opposite of what you would guess. We spell out what yea and nay meant on every vote, and we tuck the routine ones out of the way.
